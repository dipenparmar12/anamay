# External research used in the redesign

The organisation's own content (see `SITE-AUDIT.md`) is the foundation of the redesign. The facts below come from authoritative public sources. They are used only for short educational context ("what is it, where does it come from"), and every one is cited on the page where it appears.

## Facts used

| Topic | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Vedic chanting | UNESCO proclaimed the Tradition of Vedic Chanting a Masterpiece of Intangible Heritage in 2003 and inscribed it on the Representative List in 2008. It is passed on orally, using recitation techniques that keep every syllable and tonal accent unaltered. Only a small number of the many historical recitation branches survive. | UNESCO ICH, https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-vedic-chanting-00062 |
| Yoga | UNESCO inscribed Yoga on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016. | UNESCO ICH, https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/yoga-01163 |
| Transcendental Meditation | TM is practised for 20 minutes twice a day, sitting comfortably with the eyes closed, and is taught one-to-one by a certified teacher. | tm.org |
| TM research | A 2013 American Heart Association scientific statement (Brook et al., *Hypertension* 61:1360–83) concluded that TM may be considered in clinical practice to lower blood pressure. | https://doi.org/10.1161/HYP.0b013e318293645f |
| Ayurveda | One of the world's oldest medical systems and one of India's traditional health-care systems. It combines plant-based preparations, diet, exercise and lifestyle. NIH advises telling your doctor about any Ayurvedic products you use. | NCCIH, https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth |
| Kshar Sutra | The Indian Council of Medical Research ran a multicentre randomised controlled trial of Kshara Sutra versus conventional surgery for fistula-in-ano (Bombay, Chandigarh, New Delhi and Wardha), published in 1991. | *Indian J Med Res* 1991;94:177–85, PubMed 1937599 |
| Panchakarma | Classical texts describe three phases. Pūrvakarma (preparation, mainly oleation and sudation) comes first, then pradhānakarma (the main cleansing procedures) and finally paścātkarma (aftercare, including the graded diet called saṃsarjana krama, which the original site also mentions). | Charaka Saṃhitā, general Ayurvedic literature |
| Five kośas | The five sheaths (annamaya, prāṇamaya, manomaya, vijñānamaya and ānandamaya) are described in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad. The original yoga page explains them. | Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2 |
| Kausani | Mahatma Gandhi stayed at Kausani in 1929, at what is now the Anasakti Ashram. The town looks out on a panorama of about 300 km of Himalayan peaks, including Trishul, Nanda Devi and Panchachuli. | Uttarakhand tourism and travel sources |

## Corrections noted while researching

- **Motto citation:** the phrase पदं गच्छन्त्यनामयम् ends verse **2.51** of the Bhagavad Gītā (कर्मजं बुद्धियुक्ता हि … जन्मबन्धविनिर्मुक्ताः पदं गच्छन्त्यनामयम्). The live site cites it as 2.52. The redesign uses 2.51, so please confirm.
- "Anamayakosha" on the yoga page is spelled *Annamaya kośa* (the sheath of food) in the redesign.

## Visual assets

- 52 photographs come from the organisation's own site. They are resized to WebP in `website/assets/img/`.
- 23 openly licensed photographs come from Wikimedia Commons (CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA). Every image's author, licence, link and caption is recorded in `website/src/credits.json` and shown on `website/credits.html`. The files are resized, but the content is otherwise unchanged.
- Unsplash was also searched, but its API refused anonymous access, so nothing was taken from it. Images whose subject is another organisation's temple, or architecture outside the Vedic tradition, were rejected.
- The candidate lists from both search rounds are kept in `content/research/` for review.
