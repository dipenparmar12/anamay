Dinacharyā · the day

Should you miss Brahma-muhūrta…

If you miss Brahma-muhūrta, the hour or two before sunrise that is ideal for deep meditation, or doze off again afterwards, the gentle morning sun will knock at your closed eyes. You open them to the Himalaya.

Use the golden morning hours for yoga āsanas, on the roof terrace or with all the students of the Vidyapeeth. Before diving into deep meditation with everyone in the ashram, have a freshly brewed kāḍhā, our herbal tea.

Then a healthy breakfast of idli, poha or upma, with fruit to start. After breakfast, do your chores. Volunteers do their seva, and guests enjoy excursions, Panchakarma or special knowledge programmes.

Lunch brings lovely maduā (ragi) rotis with dal, vegetables, red organic rice, and freshly made buttermilk from our own cows. Even Indra cannot get it in heaven: तक्रं शक्रस्य दुर्लभम्. Then spend some time enjoying the view in silence.

At five the beautiful evening routine begins: yogāsana and group meditation, followed by a divine evening Ārati. After dinner there are knowledge programmes on different topics. And remember, the success of the next day depends on how early you go to rest.

Food at the ashram is purely Ayurvedic and vegetarian, with no processed food, refined sugar, eggs or caffeine. All grains used are gluten-free.
Organic farming

Towards complete self-sufficiency

The ashram is a proud member of WWOOF (World Wide Opportunities on Organic Farms). Local staff and WWOOF volunteers from around the world work our organic land together. Many of our vegetables come from our own polyhouses and nethouses. All our fertilisers are chemical-free, and we source much of our grain from local farmers.

Our newest Vedic Organic Greenhouse, one of three, is fully operational. Sāma Veda and Ṛg Veda are played over loudspeakers during the day, nurturing the tender plants from the most fundamental level of nature, and the pandit boys help diligently.

Vedic agriculture

An ancient way of farming

Organic farming is not new in India. Enlightened Rishis who lived in the forests understood the rhythms of nature, among them Parāśara Maharishi, and taught the art and science of cultivation according to Vedic science, with both tantra and mantra.

{{icon:seed}}

Healthy soil

Crop rotation and cover crops. Composted manure and on-farm residues are returned to the soil to maintain organic matter and fertility.

{{icon:leaf}}

Living balance

Biodiversity and balanced host–predator relationships, with no synthetic off-farm inputs.

{{icon:sun}}

Natural timing

Preventive care through planting and harvesting in tune with the season and the lunar cycles.

Goshala

Our cows

The cow barn gives fresh milk twice a day, and butter is made daily. Caring for the cows is part of daily seva, and part of what your donation supports.

The ashram aims to be self-sufficient in energy, water and food. It has a carpentry workshop, machines to process grain and press oil, and wild pear trees whose fruit becomes chutney. Every building is designed according to Vāstu, the Vedic science of architecture.
