Raja Yoga

Perfect mind–body coordination

Anāmay teaches a form of Raja Yoga. Physical postures (āsanas) are taught progressively, suited to each participant.

Breathing techniques (prāṇāyāma) lead further, to the experience of perfect mind–body coordination and a strong, healthy physiology. Because Anāmay means perfect health in enlightenment, it asks for the best of its whole field: Yoga, Āyurveda and Vāstu together.

Courses also include basic Ayurvedic lifestyle guidelines, Gandharva Veda (music and dance), and the principles of Vāstu: how to behave, enjoy and build a life in tune with natural law. Profound Vedic knowledge and experience allow the holistic understanding of true yoga, unity with the Divine, the highest in one's own consciousness.

Pañca-kośa · the five sheaths

From the body to bliss

The Upaniṣads describe five layers of our existence, from the outermost to the innermost. Yoga works through all five.

  1. १
    Annamaya kośaThe physical body, maintained by food: strengthened by āsana and a sattvic diet.
  2. २
    Prāṇamaya kośaThe sheath of vital energy. This is why prāṇāyāma is so effective.
  3. ३
    Manomaya kośaThe mind. Transcendental Meditation leads it to its most natural, simple and fulfilling state.
  4. ४
    Vijñānamaya kośaThe layer of total knowledge and wisdom, naturally experienced.
  5. ५
    Ānandamaya kośaThe basis of all: bliss, ānanda.

The five kośas are described in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad.

A world heritage

Yoga belongs to humanity

In 2016 UNESCO inscribed Yoga on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The listing recognises it as an ancient Indian practice uniting body, mind and spirit. At Anāmay it is practised as it was meant to be: as one limb of a whole Vedic life, alongside meditation, Āyurveda and the Veda.

Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — Yoga
