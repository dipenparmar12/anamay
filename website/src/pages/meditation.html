What is meditation?

Going beyond thought

Meditation is a simple, systematic technique for going to deeper and deeper levels of thinking, until the subtlest thought is transcended and the inner Self, Ātmā, is experienced.

Maharishi Mahesh Yogi taught Transcendental Meditation (TM) all over the world from 1955, reviving what Ashutosh ji calls the most important, most ancient and most effective aspect of the Vedic tradition of knowledge. Its goal is enlightenment: inner calm, a quiet state of least excitation, that remains even when we are dynamically busy.

At Anāmay, everyone meditates: the Vedic students, the Acharyas, the staff, the volunteers and the guests, together, morning and evening.

Sit comfortably, eyes closed

No special posture and no control of the mind. TM is practised for about 20 minutes, twice a day.

Effortless, not concentration

The attention is allowed to settle naturally to quieter levels of thought, using a technique learned personally.

Learned from a teacher

TM is taught one-to-one by a certified teacher, as it is in the ashram's 1-week course.

A moment of stillness

Pause for one quiet breath

Before you read on, let your attention rest with the circle as it grows and softens. Nothing to do, nothing to hold.

This is only an invitation to slow down. TM itself is not a breathing exercise. It is learned personally from a qualified teacher.

Benefits

What research has observed

Maharishi encouraged modern scientific study of this simple, effortless technique.

१

Integrated brain functioning

Increased orderliness and integration in brain functioning.

२

Deep rest

A reduced rate of respiration: the body settles as the mind settles.

३

Healthy blood pressure

A 2013 American Heart Association statement concluded TM may be considered to help lower blood pressure.

४

Better behaviour

Improved behaviour in domestic and corporate settings.

५

Coherence in society

Studies of group practice report increased coherence in the environment and reduced violence and crime.

६

Inner calm in activity

A settled, quiet state of mind that stays even when we are dynamically busy.

Sources: Anāmay Ashram; Brook RD et al., “Beyond medications and diet: alternative approaches to lowering blood pressure”, American Heart Association scientific statement, Hypertension 2013. Meditation supports, but does not replace, medical care.
