The Gurukul way

Learning the Veda as it has always been learned

In a Gurukul the student lives with the teacher and learns by listening and repeating until the knowledge becomes part of him.

The ashram has 40 to 50 students and 10 to 12 Acharyas (Vedic teachers). Students come from Bihar and Assam, from Delhi, and naturally from this region. Since October 2020 the Vidyapeeth has been officially part of Maharishi Sandipani Rashtriya Vedvidya Pratishthan. Students sit yearly examinations in their Veda and receive a monthly stipend that supports their families.

Five of the senior Sāma Veda students were sent to Shringeri in South India for final training with one of the most proficient living Sāma Veda acharyas. They will re-awaken the Sāma Veda tradition in the Himalaya, which has no Sāma Veda acharyas today.

The path of a student

From the first stotra to the yagya

Guru Paramparā Pūjā & Viṣṇu Sahasranāma

Learned in the village programme before joining the residential school.

Stotras

Gajendramokṣa, Durgā Saptaśatī and Rudrābhiṣeka are mastered at Kausani.

Choosing a Veda

After six months each student chooses Ṛg, Sāma, Yajur or Atharva Veda. Sāma and Ṛg are encouraged, as their pandits are rare.

Vyākaraṇa & Nirukta

Grammar and meaning, alongside the verses learned by heart.

Āyurveda, Vāstu, Jyotish

Sthāpatya Veda, plants and their uses with our Vaidya, and the calculation of muhūrta.

Kalpa

Advanced students learn the hows and whys of yagyas, the Vedic performances.

Inner and outer expansion

Meditation in the morning, stars at night

Alongside the Sandipani curriculum of Veda, Sanskrit grammar, general knowledge, mathematics and English, every student practises Transcendental Meditation and the TM-Sidhi programme morning and evening, with yoga āsanas and prāṇāyāma.

The Vidyapeeth has added basic astronomy and trigonometry. With a 9-inch telescope that magnifies 81 times, students learn to calculate planetary positions and see the vastness of the universe for themselves.

“We have inner expansion by practising Transcendental Meditation every day, and outer expansion by experiencing the vastness of the universe.”

Beyond the ashram

Vedic schools across Uttarakhand

After-school classes are offered in cities and villages throughout the Himalaya through the Dhanvantari Dhyan Yog Ved Vigyan Utthan Samiti, a related NGO. Children practise Transcendental Meditation and yoga āsanas together and learn selected stotras of the Vedic literature, for one hour a day.

More than 500 students are enrolled across the state. The programme is a hope for the re-enlivenment of Vedic values and a Vedic lifestyle, and most residential students first come to the ashram through it.
