Beside the ashram

A guest house in the home of the Veda

Anāmay Vedic Retreat stands next to Anāmay Ashram on the edge of Kausani, on the north-facing side of the mountain with a clear view of the high Himalaya.

Guests live alongside the ashram. They hear the Vedic chanting of the young pandits every day, join meditation and yoga, and can take Vedic and Ayurvedic rejuvenation programmes with our Vaidya. At about 2,000 m (6,000 ft) the temperature is moderate all year round, a welcome escape from the heat of the plains.

Before you come

What is the food like?

We serve organic vegetarian food, cooked without garlic, onion or chilli. A typical lunch is rice, dal, yoghurt, roti, a vegetable dish and green salad with olive oil. Dinner is usually soup, a vegetable dish, roti and sometimes rice. Breakfast is often mildly spiced rice with beans, or sweet milk-rice.

Many vegetables come from our own garden. We call them Vedic organic, because the plants hear Vedic chanting all day, live from the pandits and through loudspeakers. Milk from our own cows is served morning and evening. Some rooms have a kitchen corner where you can cook for yourself.

When is the best time of year to come?

Any time has its charm; it depends on what you want to do. For taking part in ashram life, yagyas, meditation or Panchakarma, every season is good.

If you want to use the ashram as a base for hiking in the Himalaya, autumn (October and November) is generally best. To study the flora and herbs, for example in the famous Valley of Flowers a day's journey away, the rainy season from June to September or October is better. For smaller hikes in the local Kumaon hills, even the winter months are ideal.

The mountains are clearest from October to March, and most hidden in April to June, when the plains are hot and hazy, though the early mornings can still be clear. In the rains the peaks may hide for weeks, but when the clouds part they make the most amazing scenes.

How warm or cold is it?

At 2,000 m (6,000 ft) the climate is balanced. On a few days in June the temperature has reached 34 °C, yet air-conditioning is never needed. On a few winter mornings there can be frozen water on the ground. In recent years snow has fallen for no more than a few hours a season and has not stayed more than half a day.

Winter days are usually sunny and warm enough to eat outside, and our lemon and orange trees would not survive real frost. All guest rooms have floor heating, so even Panchakarma in winter is comfortable.

What did Maharishi say about rejuvenation in the Himalaya?

Maharishi Mahesh Yogi spoke about the value of the Himalaya for one's evolution, and of his plans to build Himalayan retreats. Read his words on our Facebook page.

How do I get here?

Our driver travels regularly between Delhi and Kausani (410 km, about 10 hours) and can often bring you, or collect you from Pantnagar airport. You can also take the train to Kathgodam and a four-hour taxi ride up. All travel details
