What is Kshar Sutra?

A medicated thread that cuts and heals at once

Kshar Sutra uses a specially prepared thread, coated with herbal alkaline preparations (kṣāra), to treat anorectal disease.

The great Indian surgeon Acharya Sushruta described the use of kṣāra for anal fistula and other anorectal diseases. Acharya Chakrapani Datta and Acharya Bhav Mishra later described how to prepare the thread and treat fistula-in-ano with it. The thread acts by “herbal chemical cauterisation” and removes the disease from the body.

In 1991 the Indian Council of Medical Research published a multicentre randomised trial comparing Kshar Sutra with conventional surgery for fistula-in-ano, run in Bombay, Chandigarh, New Delhi and Wardha. Many Ayurvedic studies since have reported encouraging results.

Source: Indian J Med Res 1991;94:177–85 (ICMR multicentric trial)

What it treats

Six conditions, one gentle method

01

Piles (haemorrhoids)

Internal and external. The thread is tied at the root, the pile dries and falls away, and recurrence is reduced.

02

Fissure

Chronic fissures with sentinel piles. The kṣāra relaxes the muscle and the wound heals.

03

Fistula

Simple and complicated. The thread drains infection, is changed weekly, and cuts and heals the track.

04

Pilonidal sinus

The cavity is cleaned and treated with kṣāra paste and thread until the track heals.

05

Anal abscess

Painful, pus-filled boils around the back passage.

06

Rectal polyps

The thread is tied at the base. The polyp dries and sheds off.

Also used in anal stenosis, sentinel tags, anal incontinence, anal condyloma, anal haematoma and rectal prolapse.

Benefits over conventional surgery

Why patients choose it

  • The muscles that support the anus and control bowel movements are not cut, so the risk of incontinence is lowered.
  • The thread cuts and heals at the same time, rather than leaving a large open wound prone to infection.
  • Narrowing of the anal canal after treatment is almost never seen, and the anal sphincter is not damaged.
  • Minimal chance of recurrence and bleeding.
  • The hospital stay is only 4–5 hours, usually without antibiotic cover.
  • Patients return to routine life in 3–5 days.

How the thread works

Moderate pressure from the thread gradually cuts through the track from apex to periphery while the tissue heals behind it. The thread keeps the cavity draining, so no pocket of pus remains. The alkaline coating (pH 8–9) is anti-inflammatory and antibacterial. It chemically curettes the lining so the track can collapse and heal.

During treatment: stay active and walk, eat normal food with plenty of fibre, vegetables and water, and avoid spicy food and foods that constipate. Keep the bowel regular, avoid prolonged sitting or standing and long drives, and keep the area clean and dressed.
